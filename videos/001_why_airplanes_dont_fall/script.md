# Scene 2: The Wrong Answer (WindAndWing)

[VISUAL: Classic textbook wing diagram draws in. Arrows show air splitting at the front -- longer curved path on top, shorter straight path below. A "longer path" label appears.]

You probably learned this in school. The wing is curved on top, so air travels a longer path, moves faster, creates low pressure, and the plane gets sucked upward.

[VISUAL: A big red X stamps over the diagram. Then an upside-down airplane FadeIn from the right, flying happily.]

Sounds clean. One problem: if that were the whole story, planes could never fly upside down. But they can. Stunt pilots do it all the time.

So what is actually going on?

[VISUAL: Diagram fades out. A car appears driving along with wind streaks.]

# Scene 3: The Real Explanation Part 1 (WindAndWing continued)

[VISUAL: A hand sticks out of the car window, flat. Then it tilts upward slightly. Arrows appear showing air deflecting downward off the hand. A big upward arrow appears labeled "LIFT".]

Start with something you have done a hundred times. Hand out the car window. Flat? Nothing happens. Tilt it up just a little?

You feel a force pushing your hand up. That is lift. And it is the exact same physics keeping a 747 in the air.

[VISUAL: The hand smoothly morphs into a wing cross-section. The arrows remain, showing the continuity.]

A wing is just a fancy version of your tilted hand. It deflects air downward.

# Scene 4: Newton Enters (LiftExplained)

[VISUAL: Newton's Third Law text writes in at top. Below, the wing pushes air DOWN with blue arrows. An equal green arrow pushes the wing UP. Labels: "Action" pointing down, "Reaction" pointing up.]

Newton's third law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

Wing pushes air down. Air pushes wing up. That upward push is lift.

Simple. Brutal. Effective.

[VISUAL: The green "reaction" arrow pulses and grows. Text appears: "This is lift."]

But here is the thing -- Newton is only half the answer.

# Scene 5: Bernoulli Joins (LiftExplained continued)

[VISUAL: Wing reappears with a color gradient -- blue (low pressure) flowing above, red-orange (high pressure) below. Pressure labels animate in.]

The curved shape of the wing does something clever. Air flowing over the top speeds up and spreads out. Faster flow means lower pressure. That is Bernoulli's principle.

So you get lower pressure above the wing and higher pressure below. The wing gets pushed upward by this pressure difference too.

[VISUAL: Split screen merges -- Newton arrows on the left, Bernoulli colors on the right, both combining onto one wing diagram. Text: "The real answer: BOTH".]

The real answer? It is not Newton OR Bernoulli. It is both. Together. Always.

# Scene 6: The Myth Debunked (MythBust)

[VISUAL: The old "equal transit time" diagram reappears with a question mark. Then animated air particles show that air on top actually arrives FASTER than air on bottom -- they do NOT meet up at the trailing edge at the same time.]

Remember that textbook explanation? It relies on air splitting at the front and meeting at the back at the same time. But wind tunnel experiments show that never happens. The air on top arrives first. Every time.

[VISUAL: "Equal Transit Time" text gets crossed out with an animated strikethrough. NASA logo fades in beside text: "Debunked by NASA".]

NASA calls this the "equal transit time fallacy." Your textbook was wrong. You can officially feel smug about that.

# Scene 7: The Secret Variable (MythBust continued)

[VISUAL: A wing rotates slowly, angle of attack increasing. The lift arrow grows as the angle increases. At about 15 degrees, the smooth airflow on top breaks into turbulent swirls. The lift arrow collapses. "STALL" text flashes.]

But here is what pilots actually care about: angle of attack. Tilt the wing up more? More lift. Tilt too far? The air cannot follow the curve anymore. It separates. Lift disappears.

That is a stall. And that is the real danger -- not falling because gravity wins, but falling because you asked the wing to do too much.

# Scene 8: Recap and CTA (Outro)

[VISUAL: Clean final diagram -- wing with both Newton arrows (air down, plane up) and Bernoulli gradient (blue above, red below). Simple, elegant, complete.]

So next time you look up at a 400-ton metal tube cruising overhead, remember: it is not magic and it is not just one equation. It is Newton and Bernoulli, working together, shoving air down so the plane goes up.
